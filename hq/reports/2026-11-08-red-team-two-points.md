## 1. Data reality
Available, obtained, and in-repo. `data/pbp/` is **both** gitignored (`/data/`) and in `_config.yml` `exclude`, so it will not ship. The research memo's "not gitignored" is stale, so correct it. The shipped files under `posts/safety/analysis/` (script, CSVs, JSON) and `posts/safety/visualization/plot_safety.py` will publish as raw files. That is intended (Methods cites them) but note `results.json` embeds 140-character play descriptions. Nothing secret.

**Failure modes of the data as used:**
- **21 free kicks.** 2024–25 free-kick EP is 1.926, SD 1.007, **SE 0.22**. The 2024–25 safety net is therefore 3.93 ± 0.43 (95%). Era SEs: 0.07 (n=62), 0.09 (n=102), 0.22 (n=21). The "3.75 → 3.86 → 3.93" trend is noise-compatible; by season the free-kick EP ranges from 1.36 (2018) to 2.19 (2025). What is robust: pooled free-kick EP 1.83 (n=185, SE 0.06) exceeds kickoff EP in 2024–25 by 0.51 (bootstrap 95% CI 0.11 to 0.95), so the sign of "the free kick is worth more than a kickoff" holds, but the 2024–25 point estimate should not carry a headline.
- **Intentional safety detection is a filter, not an observation.** The 11 come from `play_type == "run"`, "Punt formation" in `desc`, ≤120 s, scorer leading. Only 1 of 11 descriptions says "intentionally". Anything outside the filter (QB-kneel variants, non-punt formations, safeties at 120+ s) is excluded by construction. "The only deliberate use of the safety is a clock trick" (Summary item 3) is therefore true by definition of the filter. Intentional grounding in the end zone (22 safeties) and other forced-choice safeties are arguably deliberate too and are classed as "earned" without discussion.
- **Real ring overlay mismatch (the critical one).** The grid samples punts from `yardline_100 >= 95` (own 1–5; script l.203) and the chart title reads "a team at its own 1–5". The 11 real intentional safeties started at (yardline_100): 82, 92, 92, 94, 99, 77, 72, 97, 77, 82, 66, i.e., own 18, 8, 8, 6, 1, 23, 28, 3, 23, 18, 34. **Only 2 of 11 started inside the own 5.** Only two cells of evidence are in the model's domain.
- The chart plots 10 rings (the 30-point lead is off the +21 axis) while the text says 11 are ringed.

## 2. Confound audit (the boring explanations)
1. **The blue corner is clock arithmetic, not win-probability modelling.** At ≤10 s `wp_after` returns the deterministic final result whenever `rem <= 0`. The punt sample's median elapsed time is 11 s and the free-kick sample's 12 s, plus 5 s assumed for the retreat. So the safety "wins" mostly because 17 s of expected elapsed time ends the game while 11 s sometimes does not. The delta is a function of two assumed numbers: the +5 s retreat and the median clocks. (Sensitivity to the +5 s is reported in section 6.)
2. **Starting-state confound for "earned".** Finding 2 says 77% of safeties come on drives that began inside the own 10, so the offense's pre-snap EP on an earned safety averages **−0.92** (median −0.88). A pick-six, by contrast, happens when the offense's pre-snap EP averages **+1.16** (n=955 defensive return TDs). The defense did not create the field position in most safeties; the punter or kick coverage did (the draft says so, then declines to split the award).
3. **Selection:** safeties are scored disproportionately by good pass-rush teams on weak offenses. The EP-based net value does not adjust for team strength; the effect is probably small but unstated.
4. **Era confound for the "widening gap".** See Fix 6.
5. **Behavioral response is entirely unmodelled** and is the largest confound of the proposed rule. Examples the draft does not discuss: intentional grounding in the end zone (22 safeties) would cost 7 instead of 2, so quarterbacks and coaches change what they do under pressure at the 1–3; punt formation from own 1–5 changes; penalty-in-end-zone safeties (offensive holding, 19–25 cases) become 7-point enforcement calls. The Objection "It makes the goal line too important" covers spot-of-ball only.

## 3. The "net value" framing (EP of next possession)
- **Legitimate as a ledger of what follows the score.** nflverse EP is an expected next-score margin, so `points + EP(next possession)` is a defensible way to price each restart. The draft is right that nflverse EPA scores a safety as flat −2 (confirmed: `ep + epa` = −2.0 on safety plays, mean −1.91).
- **But it measures the post-score state, not what the play accomplished.** On the "swing" currency (post-score value minus the defense's pre-snap baseline), using the numbers above:
  - safety, current rule: 3.93 − 0.92 = **3.0**
  - safety, under the Act: 5.54 − 0.92 = **4.6**
  - pick-six: 5.54 + 1.16 = **6.7**
  So the draft's claim that the Act makes a safety "worth the same as a touchdown" is true in net-value currency and **false** in swing currency, where even the Act leaves the safety 2.1 points short of a pick-six. The Minister may prefer either currency, but the draft must say which one it uses and why, because they point different ways on how generous the remedy is.
- **Finding 1 does not support the amendment.** "Football says 3.9" is the data's own answer to "is the safety undervalued": the scoreboard understates it by 1.9, and the free kick already pays most of it. Going to 5.54 is a normative step (the Minister's position that earned defensive scores equal touchdowns). The research memo itself concluded "keep the 2-point rule; the amendment solves a problem the data doesn't show." The draft should state the normative step openly rather than leave Finding 1's headline ("already undervalued") to imply it.
- **"More than two field goals combined"** compares 3.93 to 2 × 1.59 = 3.18, which is true, but it is a confusing unit. Say "worth 2.5 times a field goal".
- **End-game currency switch.** Finding 1 uses EP; Finding 3 uses WP; EP is not meaningful inside the last two minutes (the script excludes those restarts). The draft should say that the two findings use different currencies by design.

## 4. WP model reliability in end-game states
- **Brier 0.159 vs 0.171 is evaluated on all 37,835 scrimmage snaps in 2025**, dominated by mid-game states. It says nothing about calibration inside 60 s. No calibration plot or end-game slice is shown.
- **Sparse training region.** `min_samples_leaf=200` with 12 features; states with <60 s left, ≤1 timeout difference and a specific field position are far too rare for each leaf, so the model extrapolates from monotone constraints. Ties are dropped (`result != 0`).
- **Reported noise is ±3 pts "away from the final seconds"**, but the blue cells that matter are in the final seconds, where the model is least tested and where the result is mostly determined by `rem <= 0` clock logic (section 2.1). Say that the blue corner comes from clock arithmetic, not the model, and show it: the same corner appears with a trivial WP rule ("did the game end?").
- **Final model is refit on all data including the 2025 holdout** (`m.fit(d[FEATS], d.won)`), fine for counterfactuals but the Methods say "validated on 2025" without noting the refit.
- **Rule-panel kickoff sampling.** Late states borrow kickoffs only where the kicker trails by 1–8 (714 plays, 5.5% kicker recovery). Everything else, including situations where the kicker now *leads* with seconds left, uses ordinary 2024–25 kickoffs from the first 50 minutes. Direction is not in doubt (conceding 7 points loses), but "43–92" is false precision. Present as "loses nearly all of its win probability".
- **The right-panel result is a tautology.** Any rule that hands the opponent ~7 points abolishes the intentional safety. The WP model is not needed to establish it, and the draft should not spend a paragraph on it as a finding.

2. Summary, last sentence: "abolishes the intentional safety outright" is stated as a benefit; section 4 above explains why it is a tautology. Present it as a consequence, not a finding.
3. The Objection "Sample sizes" crams five numbers into one paragraph. A deadpan list reads better, and clarity gains there outweigh any bit.

5. The Summary calls the safety "the smallest score on offer"; an extra point is worth 1. Say "the smallest score from open play" or "tied for smallest touchdown-less score"; the sentence is a rule claim the data does not touch.

## 8. Facts about NFL history or rules the data does not support
- "Two points have not been revised in living memory": unsupported by the data; not obviously wrong, but cut or hedge it.
- "Each round of kickoff reform has handed the receiving team better field position": the data shows receiver start moved from 78.3 to 75.1 to 70.3 (`yl`, distance to goal), with jumps between 2015→2016 (78.3→75.0) and 2023→2024 (74.7→70.4). It does not say what the rules were. Name the rule changes with their years or say "kickoffs landed progressively closer to midfield".
- **Causal error:** the sentence "makes the safety's free possession relatively more valuable" is backwards. Kickoff rules do not touch the free kick. The safety's net rose only 0.17 (within noise), while the FG net fell 0.66. The gap widened because the **field goal** got cheaper, not because the safety got dearer.
- "The rules currently permit ... a recognized form of strategy" is a rules claim; fine, but it is the Minister's wording, not the data's.
- The Act's "try" and "kickoff rules then in force" are asserted without checking that the try would be taken by the defense-turned-scorer's offense from the 2 or 3; Section 2 is silent on who lines up.
- "Free kick from its own 20": supported (180/185).
